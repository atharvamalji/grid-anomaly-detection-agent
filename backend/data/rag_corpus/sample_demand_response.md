## Demand Spikes

Sudden increases in system demand — driven by extreme weather (heat waves,
winter storms) or unexpected load events — can strain generation and
transmission capacity. Balancing authorities monitor forecast deviations and
compare real-time demand against historical same-hour baselines to detect
abnormal load growth early.

## N-1 Contingency Events

Reliable grid operation requires the system to withstand the loss of any
single major component (a generator, transformer, or transmission line)
without cascading failures. Operators maintain reserve margins so that an
N-1 event does not force involuntary load shedding.

## Load-Shedding Procedures

When available capacity cannot meet demand despite conservation appeals and
reserve deployment, operators may implement controlled, rotating load
shedding to prevent uncontrolled cascading outages. This is a measure of
last resort, used only after other options (demand response, emergency
purchases, voltage reduction) are exhausted.
