# NERC Reliability Standard EOP-002 — Capacity and Energy Emergencies

**Source:** NERC Reliability Standard EOP-002-3.1 ("Capacity and Energy
Emergencies"). **Status: inactive, superseded by EOP-011** (see
`nerc_eop011_emergency_operations.md`). Included here because its requirement
structure is the direct predecessor to EOP-011's emergency-alert framework and
is still widely referenced for its clearer breakdown of capacity vs. energy
emergency response steps. Summarized via NERCipedia standards reference, since
NERC's own PDF hosting blocked automated retrieval in this session.

## Purpose

Ensures that Reliability Coordinators and Balancing Authorities prepare
adequately for capacity and energy emergencies affecting the interconnected
power system. Applies to Balancing Authorities, Reliability Coordinators, and
Load-Serving Entities.

## Core Requirements

- **Authority and Responsibility (R1):** Reliability Coordinators and Balancing
  Authorities must have clear decision-making power to implement necessary
  actions for system reliability during emergencies.
- **Emergency Planning (R2):** Balancing Authorities must execute the actions
  outlined in their capacity and energy emergency plans when circumstances
  warrant intervention.
- **Communication Protocols (R3):** During active emergencies, entities
  experiencing capacity or energy shortfalls must communicate conditions to
  their Reliability Coordinator and neighboring Balancing Authorities.
- **Anticipatory Actions (R4):** Entities expecting emergencies must activate
  all available generation, delay maintenance, schedule advance interchange
  purchases, and prepare for potential load reduction.
- **Frequency Bias Limitations (R5):** A deficient Balancing Authority may
  only use Interconnection frequency-bias assistance for the time needed to
  implement corrective actions.
- **Compliance Measures (R6):** When control performance standards cannot be
  met, Balancing Authorities must deploy available capacity, operating
  reserves, and load interruption procedures progressively.
- **Emergency Escalation (R7-R8):** Entities unable to resolve an emergency
  must shed firm load and request their Reliability Coordinator declare an
  Energy Emergency Alert through established procedures.

## Energy Emergency Alert Levels (EOP-002 framing)

- **Alert 1** — All available resources committed; non-firm sales curtailed.
- **Alert 2** — Load management procedures implemented; firm interruption
  pending.
- **Alert 3** — Firm load interruption either imminent or underway.

## Relevance to Demand Anomalies

This standard is the historical basis for the progressive-escalation model
(situational awareness → advisory → warning → event) that both NERC's current
EOP-011 and MISO's own operating-procedure escalation ladder implement. A
sustained demand spike well above forecast and above rolling baseline is the
triggering condition this standard's R4 "anticipatory actions" and R7-R8
"emergency escalation" requirements are designed around — the expectation is
that a Balancing Authority is already activating available generation and
preparing demand-reduction options well before load shedding becomes
necessary.
