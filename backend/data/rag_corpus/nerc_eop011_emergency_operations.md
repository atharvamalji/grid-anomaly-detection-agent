# NERC Reliability Standard EOP-011 — Emergency Operations

**Source:** NERC (North American Electric Reliability Corporation), Reliability
Standard EOP-011 ("Emergency Operations"). Current active version is EOP-011-4;
content below is summarized from EOP-011-1 (superseded, via NERCipedia
standards reference) since NERC's own PDF hosting blocked automated retrieval
in this session (HTTP 403). Verify against the current EOP-011-4 text at
https://www.nerc.com/globalassets/standards/reliability-standards/eop/eop-011-4.pdf
before treating specific requirement numbers as authoritative — the
requirement structure has been revised across versions; the EEA-level concept
and general emergency-planning intent described here are stable across
revisions.

## Purpose

The standard ensures that Transmission Operators and Balancing Authorities
develop coordinated emergency mitigation plans, addressing effects of
operating emergencies within Reliability Coordinator Areas.

## Key Requirements (structure, EOP-011-1)

- **Transmission Operators (R1):** Must maintain reviewed Operating Plans
  including notification procedures, outage management, system
  reconfiguration, generation redispatch, manual load shedding provisions, and
  extreme weather considerations.
- **Balancing Authorities (R2):** Must develop plans addressing Capacity and
  Energy Emergencies, encompassing resource management, voluntary demand
  appeals, interruptible load use, manual load shedding, and weather impacts.
- **Reliability Coordinators (R3-R6):** Must review submitted plans within 30
  days, identify reliability risks, and ensure timely emergency notifications
  to neighboring entities within 30 minutes of receiving alerts.

## Energy Emergency Alert (EEA) Levels

- **EEA 1** — All available generation resources are committed to meet firm
  load, firm transactions, and reserve commitments; the Balancing Authority is
  concerned about sustaining its required Contingency Reserves.
- **EEA 2** — The Balancing Authority is no longer able to provide its
  expected energy requirements and is an energy-deficient Balancing Authority;
  load management procedures activate.
- **EEA 3** — The energy-deficient Balancing Authority is unable to meet
  minimum Contingency Reserve requirements; firm load interruption is
  imminent or underway (rotating blackouts as a last resort).

## Relevance to Demand Anomalies

An Energy Emergency Alert is an emergency procedure, not a daily operating
practice, and is not an alternative to compliance with NERC Reliability
Standards. A sustained demand deviation or spike that pushes a Balancing
Authority toward its reserve margin is exactly the kind of condition this
standard's emergency planning requirements are designed to address —
Transmission Operators and Balancing Authorities are required to have
pre-built plans (notification chains, redispatch options, manual load-shed
provisions) specifically for extreme-weather-driven demand emergencies.
